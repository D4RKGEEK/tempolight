The runtime ([`packages/server/src/runtime.js`](https://github.com/D4RKGEEK/tempolight/blob/main/packages/server/src/runtime.js)) owns the frame loop, the music
sources, the devices and their senders. It is environment-free: every socket, HTTP call and sound card comes in through
`io` (on a PC or Pi [`node-io.js`](https://github.com/D4RKGEEK/tempolight/blob/main/packages/server/src/node-io.js), which re-exports
[`packages/outputs`](https://github.com/D4RKGEEK/tempolight/blob/main/packages/outputs/src/index.js) and `capture.js`; in the online demo the pretend devices of
`apps/web/demo.js`). The frame loop itself is described in [architecture](/architecture#the-frame-loop).

## Device kinds and their senders

A device in `tempolight.json` (`devices[]`) has `id`, `name`, `host`, `ddpPort` (the port, whatever the protocol),
`leds`, `enabled` and a `kind`; no `kind` means WLED. `rebuild()` picks one sender per device:

| Kind | Added with `POST /api/devices` | Sender (packages/outputs/src) | Wire | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| WLED (no kind) | `{host, name?, ddpPort?, leds?, force?}`; the LED count is read from `/json/info` | `DDPSender` (`ddp.js`) | DDP over UDP 4048, 480 pixels (1,440 bytes) per packet, push flag on the last | `protocol: "e131"` or `"artnet"` (with `universe`) switches it to `E131Sender` (`e131.js`, UDP 5568 / 6454, 170 pixels per universe) for WLED set to those, or Falcon/FPP controllers |
| Virtual | `{virtual: true, leds?}` | none | nothing is sent | shows in the preview; for trying the app without hardware |
| `govee` | `{host, kind: "govee", goveeMode: "color"\|"segments", leds?}` | `GoveeSender` (`govee.js`) | UDP 4003: `colorwc` (one colour, ~10/s) or the "razer" stream (a colour per segment) | not health-checked (Govee answers on a shared port) |
| `openrgb` | `{host, kind: "openrgb", index, ddpPort?}` | `OpenRGBSender` (`openrgb.js`) | OpenRGB SDK, TCP 6742 | one OpenRGB device per Tempolight device; connections to the same server are shared |
| `nanoleaf` | `{host, kind: "nanoleaf", token?}` (hold the power button first) | `NanoleafSender` (`nanoleaf.js`) | HTTP 16021 to pair and switch on `extControl` v2, then one UDP packet per frame to 60222 | panels are placed where they really are (`panelPoints`) |
| `hue` | `{host, kind: "hue", token?}` (press the link button first) | `HueSender` (`hue.js`) | the bridge's REST API: about 10 light commands a second in total, lights in turn, only on change | `PATCH {hueFast: true, hueArea?}` switches to `HueEntertainmentSender` (`hue-entertainment.js`): DTLS 1.2 PSK (`dtls.js`) to UDP 2100, up to 50 updates a second for the lights of an entertainment area; falls back to REST when streaming fails |
| `lifx`, `wiz`, `kasa`, `yeelight`, `twinkly` | `{host, kind, name?, force?}` | from the `LIGHT_KINDS` table (`lights.js`) | LIFX LAN UDP 56700, WiZ UDP 38899, Kasa TCP/UDP 9999, Yeelight TCP 55443 (music mode), Twinkly HTTP + UDP 7777 | one table entry per kind: `probe`, `sender`, `check`, `discover`, `options` |
| `dmx` | `{host, kind: "dmx", protocol?: "artnet"\|"e131", universe?}` | `E131Sender` with `channels: 512` | one full DMX universe | fixtures are added with `POST /api/devices/:id/fixtures`; see [DMX](#dmx) |

Each sender has the same shape: `send(Uint8Array rgb)`, `close()`, a `sent` counter, and for kinds that can switch
off by themselves `power(on)`. Smart lights and Hue/Nanoleaf/Govee get `power()` when the lights are switched; WLED
gets `setState(host, {on})` over its JSON API.

## rebuild and rebuildEffects

`rebuild()` runs after anything that changes the room or the devices (adding, editing, removing a device or shape, a
preset, a restore, a reset):

1. `buildLayout(shapes)` (all LEDs, see [director and effects](/internals/director-and-effects#the-layout-every-effect-reads)),
   `createEffects(L, {custom})` and `createDirector(L, fx)`, keeping the director's settings.
2. `segmentRanges(shapes)`: each shape's LED range on its controller.
3. For each device, a **key** of everything its sender is built from (host, port, protocol, universe, Govee mode and
   segments, LED count, OpenRGB index, Nanoleaf token and panels, Hue user, lights and fast-mode area, smart-light
   options). Only when the key changes is a new sender made (the new one first, so a connection shared with the old
   one, OpenRGB, stays open), so editing a shape doesn't reconnect anything. Senders of removed devices are closed.
4. Buffers sized to the highest LED used, and `syncDirector()`.

`rebuildEffects()` runs when custom effects change: only the effects and the director are made again (not the
layout or the senders), and what the director was doing carries over (the effect showing, the palette step, pads like
blackout and strobe).

`syncDirector()` copies `show` and `settings` into `director.settings` (mode, effect, style, palette, everyBeats, speed,
ambient, moodColours, safe, dropFlash, prefs, params, layer, zones), applying night mode (warm palette, safe on, no drop
flash).

## Health and latency

`healthLoop()` every 10 s checks every device (`checkDevice`), and `POST /api/devices/:id/check` checks one:

- WLED: `GET /json/info` (2.5 s timeout): `online`, `rssi`, `signal`, `version`, `ledsReported`, `fps` (what it
  shows), `live` (whether it shows our stream), `power` and `maxPower` (its own current limit). A device added with 0
  LEDs takes the reported count.
- Smart lights: the kind's `check()`; Hue: the light list (and, every 5 minutes, the entertainment areas for the
  Devices page); Nanoleaf: its info; OpenRGB: the device list. DMX and Govee aren't asked (DMX nodes don't answer);
  virtual devices are always online.
- Each round trip is kept (`noteRtt`: the best of the last 6).
- A WLED controller that went offline is looked for **by name** with mDNS (at most every 5 minutes): when the router gave
  it a new address, the device's host is updated.

The **latency meter** ([`latency.js`](https://github.com/D4RKGEEK/tempolight/blob/main/packages/server/src/latency.js)) is computed per device for
`status.devices[]`:

```
latencyMs = audioMs + analysisMs + lightMs - offsetMs      lightMs = networkMs + frameMs, or the device's lightLatencyMs
```

| Part | Value |
| --- | --- |
| `audioMs` | live from a browser: the browser's own estimate (input latency + its 512-sample chunk + half the WebSocket round trip); from the sound card: the chunk length; songs: 0 (their clock already allows for the speakers) |
| `analysisMs` | 23 for live audio (the analyser's clock is the middle of its last 1024-sample frame), else 0; 0 when the analyser gives its newest sample's time (`an.sampleT`) |
| `networkMs` | half the best HTTP round trip (UDP needs no answer); 5 when not measured yet (DMX, Govee, a new device) |
| `frameMs` | the device's own update time (Hue 100, Hue fast 20, Nanoleaf 33, Govee 33, LIFX 50, WiZ 100, Kasa 100, Yeelight 50, Twinkly 40), else one frame at our rate |
| `offsetMs` | `settings.syncOffsetMs` |

`latencyLevel` is `good` under 40 ms, `ok` under 80 ms, else `slow`. `warnings[]` (`kind`, `text`): `offline`,
`wifi` (signal under 45% or -72 dBm; very weak under 25% or -82 dBm), `slow` (over 60 ms each way), `notlive` (WLED
isn't showing the stream after 4 s: "Receive UDP realtime" is off), `frames` (WLED shows fewer than 60% of the frames
we send), `power` (WLED's limit, or ours, is holding brightness back).

## Light timing

Lights are late by different amounts: WLED about 20 ms, LIFX 55, Hue basic and WiZ about 105. Without help a kick
shows on WLED and on Hue 80 ms apart. When the beat is known ahead, the runtime lines them up:

1. **Lookahead.** Each frame is drawn once, for `now + L`, where `L` is the largest `lightMs` among the lights that
   show something now (enabled, not virtual, not offline), at most `LOOKAHEAD_CAP_MS` (250). A slower light is late
   by the rest. Live, the sound's way in (`audioMs + analysisMs`) is added too. So is half a frame (a light shows a
   frame for one frame time; its middle counts) and the render time.
2. **Hold.** Each light's frame is held back by `L - lightMs` in a short queue (`queueOut`, `flushOutputs`: one
   timer sends whatever is due). The slowest light gets its frame at once. So every light shows the same moment of
   the music at the same time.
3. **Trim.** `settings.syncOffsetMs` (and `syncOffsets` per sound output) stays what it was: a trim on top, for the
   speakers' own delay. It is added to the music clock as before.

Drawing once matters: effects keep state from frame to frame and start again when the time jumps back
(`effects.js` `st()`). So the plan is worked out 4 times a second and `L` moves at most 1 ms a frame; it only jumps
when the source changes (a new clock anyway). The preview and phones are held back like a light 40 ms late.

"Known ahead" means a song, a remembered song (beat map), live audio with **Predict beats** on, DJ Link, MIDI clock,
Ableton Link, the metronome, and the ambient look. Live audio with prediction off is **reactive**: nothing is drawn
ahead or held back, and each light shows what was heard as fast as it can. Pads (flash, strobe) and camera mapping
aren't drawn ahead; pads are held back with the lights, mapping goes out at once.

A light's own delay is estimated (`lightLatency()`), or set by hand: `PATCH /api/devices/:id {lightLatencyMs: 0-500}`
(`null` goes back to the estimate). `status.timing` has the mode, `lookaheadMs`, `leadMs` and the frame loop's
interval and jitter; each `status.devices[].timing` has `lightMs`, `auto`, `holdMs` and `shownMs` (the delay left,
what the meter shows: about 0 when lined up, the trim aside).

The frame loop is a chain of timeouts against deadlines (`frameTick`): the next frame is due one frame time after the
last was due, and the timeout asks for what is left. A loop more than a frame behind starts again from now. With a
busy event loop the frame interval's standard deviation went from 6.5 ms (setInterval) to 4.4 ms, and the worst late
frame from 109 ms to 17 ms; idle, from 1.8 ms to 0.8 ms.

DDP and the smart-light protocols carry no timestamps, so this can't be done on the controllers: the waiting is ours.

## Power limiter

Per device, every frame, after its buffer is filled (WLED, E1.31 and other LED kinds):

```
mA = size + sum(bytes) * mAperLed / 765          // mAperLed default 60: one LED at full white; ~1 mA idle per LED
if (maxAmps > 0 && mA > maxAmps * 1000) scale every byte by (maxAmps * 1000 - size) / (sum * mAperLed / 765)
```

`maxAmps` (0 = off, up to 500) and `mAperLed` (5-200) are set with `PATCH /api/devices/:id`. `status.devices[].amps`
is the estimate smoothed over about 5 frames, `limited` says it is cutting now. This runs on our side, so it also
covers protocols where the controller has no limiter of its own.

## Mapping mode

Camera mapping (the Room page's camera screen, `apps/web/mapper.js` with the pure `mapping.js`) lights one LED at a
time and finds it in the camera picture. The server side is `runtime.mapLight(dev, led, level, owner)`, reached by
`POST /api/map {device, led, level?}` or the WebSocket message `{type: "map", device, led}` (admin):

- while mapping, every device's buffer is black except LED `led` of device `dev` at `level` (20-255, default 200);
  `led: -1` keeps everything dark (for a reference picture). For Govee in segment mode the whole segment lights, or it
  would be too dim to see;
- a mapping ends by itself 5 s after the last message, at `{end: true}`, or as soon as the WebSocket that started it
  closes (`endMap(owner)`); then black is sent for 600 ms when the power is off;
- the browser turns the positions it found into a `points` shape and saves the room with `PUT /api/room`.

## DMX

A DMX device is one universe on an Art-Net or sACN node; its fixtures are points of the device's `points` shape, so
the effects colour them like LEDs. [`fixtures.js`](https://github.com/D4RKGEEK/tempolight/blob/main/packages/server/src/fixtures.js) turns those colours and the
music into channel values with the profiles in [`packages/outputs/src/dmx.js`](https://github.com/D4RKGEEK/tempolight/blob/main/packages/outputs/src/dmx.js):

| Profile kind | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| par, dimmer (`rgb`, `rgbw`, `rgba`, `dimmer`) | the colour (or brightness) of its point |
| moving head (`moving-head`, `-16`, `-wheel`, `-wheel-16`) | moves on the beat (`pattern`: circle, figure8, sweep, point at `target`, still; `cycle` beats per loop), colour from its point, brighter on drops, gobo changes in drops |
| `strobe` | only on the strobe pad and the first 2 s of a drop |
| `laser` | only during a drop (its first `laserMaxSec` seconds); off until `settings.dmx.lasers` |
| `fog` | a `fogBurst` s burst `fogLead` s before a drop the analysis knows (or at the drop), at least `fogCooldown` s apart and at most `fogMaxDuty` of any 5 minutes; off until `settings.dmx.fog`; the fog pad holds it (at most 10 s) |
| `custom` | a channel list (`red`, `green`, `blue`, `dimmer`, `pan`, `tilt`, `fixed:<value>`, ...; `GET /api/dmx/profiles`) |

Strobes and lasers never run in safe or night mode. The runtime sends a DMX universe about 40 times a second
whenever the device is enabled (DMX nodes hold the last values, so stopping could leave fog on), zeros for 2 s after
it is disabled, and zeros at shutdown. `GET /api/dmx` shows every channel's live value.

## Beat grids from outside

- **Pro DJ Link** (`prolink.js`, UDP 50001, `settings.djlink`): CDJ/XDJ beat packets. One deck leads: a player beats the
  mixer, a deck the mixer says is on air beats one that isn't, and the deck leading keeps the grid while it plays.
  The grid follows beats smoothly (25% of the error) and re-anchors on a jump; the beat counter keeps counting across
  deck changes.
- **MIDI clock** (`beatsources.js`, `midiclock.js`): from a browser's Web MIDI input over the WebSocket; one browser at
  a time.
- **Ableton Link** (`ableton-link.js`, listen-only, UDP 20808, `settings.link`).

`beats.grid(now, t)` gives `{P, off, barOff, bpm, kind, label}` for the first one running (MIDI clock before Link;
DJ Link is checked before both). With live audio the grid replaces the analyser's; without, a `metronomeView` runs on
it.

## Performance modes

[`perf.js`](https://github.com/D4RKGEEK/tempolight/blob/main/packages/server/src/perf.js): `settings.performance` is `auto` (default), `full` or `lite`.

| | full | lite |
| --- | --- | --- |
| frame rate | `settings.fps` (10-120, default 60) | at most 30 |
| preview to browsers | 30 fps | 15 fps |
| phones | 30 a second | 15 a second |
| status messages | every 250 ms | every 500 ms |
| live analyser | everything | chroma every 2nd frame, vocal estimate every 2nd hop |
| static files | brotli 11 | brotli 9 |

`auto` starts in lite on a weak computer (`weakHost`: a Raspberry Pi Zero, 1, 2 or 3, a 32-bit ARM, one core, or two
cores under 1.6 GHz) and steps down to lite when the frame loop can't keep up (average work over 70% of the frame
time, or under 80% of the target fps while busy, for two 5 s windows in a row). `perf.acc` keeps the milliseconds
spent in `analysis`, `memory`, `render`, `output` and `broadcast`; `status.perf` shows `{mode, setting, why, fps,
frameMs, cpu}`. `node scripts/bench.js` reports them per second (see [testing](/internals/testing#bench-and-smoke)).

## Other outputs

- **WLED audio sync** (`settings.audioSync`, `audiosync.js`): with live audio, a 44-byte "V2" packet to multicast
  239.0.0.1:11988 at most every 20 ms (16 bands, level, peak, the dominant frequency), so WLED's own sound-reactive
  effects can run on controllers that aren't streamed to.
- **Segments**: `POST /api/devices/:id/segments` writes the room's LED ranges into WLED as segments (`setSegments`,
  `stop` is exclusive).
- **Discovery** (`POST /api/discover`): WLED by mDNS (`_wled._tcp.local`, with a unicast-reply fallback when 5353 is
  taken) and a scan of the local /24 for `/json/info`; Govee by multicast; OpenRGB on this computer; and every smart-light
  kind's `discover()`.

The wire formats are in [protocols.md](/protocols).
